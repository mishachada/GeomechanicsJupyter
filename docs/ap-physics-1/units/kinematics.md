# Kinematics Unit Overview

Kinematics describes motion without considering the forces that cause it. This outline highlights essential concepts for AP Physics 1 along with a worked example to model problem-solving steps.

## Essential Objectives
- Represent motion using position, velocity, and acceleration graphs.
- Translate between verbal descriptions, diagrams, and equations of motion.
- Apply constant-acceleration equations to one-dimensional motion.
- Analyze projectile motion in two dimensions using vector components.

## Vocabulary Check
- **Displacement ($\Delta x$)** — change in position.
- **Average velocity ($\bar{v}$)** — displacement divided by elapsed time.
- **Instantaneous velocity** — slope of the position-time graph at a point.
- **Acceleration ($a$)** — rate of change of velocity.

## Worked Example: Solving for Launch Speed
A ball is rolled off a lab table that is $0.90\ \text{m}$ tall. It lands $0.45\ \text{m}$ away from the table's edge. What horizontal launch speed did the ball have?

### Step 1 — Analyze Vertical Motion
Use $y = y_0 + v_{0y} t + \frac{1}{2} a_y t^2$. With $y_0 = 0.90\ \text{m}$, $y = 0$, $v_{0y} = 0$, and $a_y = -9.8\ \text{m/s}^2$:

$$0 = 0.90 + 0 - 4.9 t^2 \Rightarrow t = \sqrt{\frac{0.90}{4.9}} \approx 0.43\ \text{s}.$$

### Step 2 — Analyze Horizontal Motion
Horizontal velocity is constant: $x = v_{0x} t$. Rearranging gives $v_{0x} = \frac{x}{t} = \frac{0.45}{0.43} \approx 1.0\ \text{m/s}$.

### Step 3 — State the Result
The ball leaves the table at approximately $1.0\ \text{m/s}$ horizontally. Emphasize that vertical and horizontal motions are independent, connected only by the shared time of flight.
