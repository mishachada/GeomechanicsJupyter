# SAT Reading Strategies

Mastering the SAT Reading section requires both efficient pacing and an analytical approach to passages. Use the checklist below to stay grounded during practice sessions.

## Before You Read
- Skim the questions first and mark any that reference specific line numbers.
- Note the passage type (literary, historical, scientific) to anticipate tone and structure.

## Active Reading Framework
1. **Annotate purposefully** — jot down paragraph roles (e.g., *introduce claim*, *provide evidence*).
2. **Track transitions** — conjunctions like "however" often signal contrasting evidence or a shift in argument.
3. **Quantify comparisons** — when data is involved, rewrite statements numerically. For example, if a passage references a growth rate of $r=\frac{15}{3}$ percent per year, reduce it to $r = 5\%$ to prevent arithmetic errors later.

## Answering Questions
- **Evidence pairs**: Answer the evidence-based question first; confirm the cited lines support your choice.
- **Vocabulary in context**: Replace the target word with your own synonym and check against answer choices.
- **Data questions**: Translate chart or table values into mini-equations. If a chart shows a linear relationship described by $y = mx + b$, quickly estimate $m$ by comparing two clear points.

!!! tip "Practice cadence"
    Alternate between timed sets and untimed review. In untimed sessions, articulate *why* each wrong answer fails — this reflection locks in the strategy.

## Reflection Prompts
- Which passage type slows you down the most?
- What evidence question strategy works best for you?
- How will you adjust your pacing for the next practice test?
